1. Executive Summary

Gotabgaa Australia is the national coordinating body for Kalenjin community associations already operating across Australian states and territories. It is a distinct association that operates independently — not an affiliate, subsidiary, or chapter of Gotabgaa International (USA). It does not replace state associations. Instead, it provides a unified national identity, shared programs, financial coordination where appropriate, and a single voice for the Australian Kalenjin diaspora.

Key message: State associations own community life on the ground. National owns what only a country-wide body can do — unity across states, national programs, national brand, and representation of Gotabgaa Australia in Australia and abroad.

This document uses a federation model (national board + state chairs and representatives) suited to the Australian context, where associations already exist in NSW, Queensland, Victoria, South Australia, Western Australia, and other jurisdictions. Structural ideas may parallel other diaspora organisations (including the US-based Gotabgaa International website) for reference only — with no governance, financial, or legal link to that body.

2. Background & Context

2.1 Reference model — Gotabgaa International (USA, separate organisation)

Gotabgaa International is a separate US-based non-profit (501(c)(3) in Georgia) registered in 2000. It is not the parent or governing body of Gotabgaa Australia. Its website describes a useful reference for how diaspora chapters can be organised — national leadership team, state chapters with Chairperson and Chapter REP, and an Oversight Council — but Australia adopts its own constitution, finances, and leadership.

2.2 Australian context

In Australia, Kalenjin community activity is already organised at state level — gatherings, sports days, welfare support, cultural events (including Taunet Nelel celebrations), and city-based networks in Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth, Adelaide, and Canberra. A national body is needed to:

  • Present one coherent “Gotabgaa Australia” identity domestically and internationally
  • Coordinate cross-state events and national programs under Australian governance
  • Prevent duplication, competition, and confusion between state groups
  • Register and represent the national association under Australian law where appropriate
  • Steward national-level funds (e.g. benevolence, scholarships) where pooling adds value

2.3 Legal status — independent association

Gotabgaa Australia should clarify its legal form (e.g. incorporated association under state law, or unincorporated federation with a lead state entity). Its constitution and policies are authoritative for Australia only. There is no affiliate agreement, reporting obligation, or shared treasury with Gotabgaa International. Members who also participate in other diaspora groups (including GI) do so in their personal capacity unless a separate voluntary arrangement exists.

5. National Leadership Board — Roles & Responsibilities

5.1 National President

  • Chief spokesperson for Gotabgaa Australia domestically and internationally
  • Chairs National Board meetings and Annual General Meeting (AGM)
  • Sets annual priorities with the board; approves major national initiatives
  • Represents Gotabgaa Australia to government, media, and community partners in Australia
  • Ensures national activities remain non-political and non-partisan

5.2 National Vice President

  • Acts for the President in absence; supports succession planning
  • Leads cross-state initiatives (e.g. national cultural calendar, youth programs)
  • Mediates between state chapters when coordination issues arise
  • Supports membership growth and new chapter formation (e.g. ACT, TAS, NT)

5.3 Secretary General

  • Maintains national constitution, policies, and official records
  • Maintains correspondence with state chapters and external bodies in Australia
  • Oversees national membership register (or coordinates with state registers)
  • Prepares agendas, minutes, and annual reports for board and AGM
  • Manages official email, document storage, and compliance filings

5.4 National Coordinator

  • Coordinates national programs: education, benevolence, outreach, cultural fete
  • Maintains master events calendar across all states
  • Liaison between national board and state Chairpersons / REPs
  • Tracks program outcomes (members served, funds disbursed, events held)
  • Aligns Australian programs with national policy and member needs

5.5 National Treasurer

  • Manages national bank accounts; maintains transparent financial records
  • Oversees national benevolence fund, donations, and grant applications
  • Prepares budgets and financial reports for board and AGM
  • Defines financial rules for state chapters (reporting, pooling, audit)

5.6 Term limits and accountability (recommended)

  • Board terms: 2–3 years, staggered elections to retain continuity
  • Maximum two consecutive terms per role (configurable in constitution)
  • Annual performance review by Chapter Council (no-confidence pathway if needed)
  • All officers subject to code of conduct and financial transparency rules

6.2 State Chairperson — responsibilities

  • Lead state association according to state constitution and national policies
  • Organise local meetings, welfare response, sports days, cultural gatherings
  • Maintain state membership list and local finances (separate from national accounts)
  • Report quarterly to National Coordinator: events, membership, finances summary
  • Appoint city coordinators within the state where helpful (Sydney lead, etc.)

6.3 Chapter Representative (REP) — responsibilities

  • Attend National Chapter Council meetings (quarterly minimum)
  • Voice state members’ concerns and priorities at national level
  • Communicate national decisions, calendar, and programs back to state members
  • Vote on national policy, budget, and program approval where council has authority
  • May be same person as Chairperson in small chapters, but separate roles preferred

6.4 Formation of new chapters

A new state or territory chapter may be recognised when:

  1. A minimum number of committed members is documented (e.g. 15–25 families — set in constitution)
  2. Interim Chair and REP are nominated
  3. National Board approves recognition at a council meeting
  4. Chapter adopts national branding and reporting standards

7. National Oversight / Advisory Council

Following a non-executive Oversight Council model (as used by some diaspora organisations), Australia should establish a small council — typically 5–9 members — including:

  • Founding members of early Australian Kalenjin / Gotabgaa groups
  • Elders and cultural custodians (language, ceremony, heritage)
  • Pastoral or community welfare advisors where appropriate
  • Optional: professional advisors (legal, accounting) on advisory basis

Powers: advise only — no day-to-day executive authority. May review disputes, constitutional changes, and major cultural or ethical questions. Reports to National Board and AGM.

10. Programs & Events Coordination

10.1 National programs

  • Education & mentorship — adapting to Australian schools and workplaces
  • Benevolence fund — member-supported assistance during loss or hardship
  • Community outreach — settlement, health awareness, African-Australian partnerships
  • Cultural fete / Taunet Nelel — annual celebration of Kalenjin heritage

10.2 Events calendar governance

  1. States propose dates to National Coordinator by quarterly deadline
  2. National publishes consolidated calendar on website
  3. Major national events (e.g. annual cultural gathering) receive national branding and board approval
  4. Conflicting dates resolved by Chapter Council vote if needed

10.3 Taunet Nelel and cross-state celebrations

Events such as Taunet Nelel may rotate host state annually or be coordinated as a national program with a host chapter — national sets standards (cultural authenticity, safety, finances); host state executes logistics.

11. Finance, Benevolence & Fundraising

11.1 Two-tier financial model

  • State accounts: local donations, local event revenue, local welfare disbursement
  • National accounts: national campaigns, scholarships, national event surplus

Never commingle without documented transfer and dual approval (state treasurer + national treasurer).

11.2 Benevolence workflow (recommended)

  1. Member in need contacts state chapter
  2. State welfare lead assesses and may approve local support from state fund
  3. If amount exceeds state threshold, state refers to national benevolence committee
  4. National treasurer disburses per Australian benevolence policy; Secretary General records decision

11.3 Transparency

  • Annual financial summary published at AGM (national + aggregated state)
  • Internal audit or external review every 2–3 years for national accounts
  • Public donation page with clear purpose statements

14. Independent Status & Gotabgaa International (Reference)

Important: Gotabgaa Australia is not an affiliate, chapter, or subsidiary of Gotabgaa International (USA). It is a distinct association with its own leadership, constitution, finances, and programs. It operates independently.

14.1 What independence means

  • Gotabgaa Australia’s board is accountable to its members and Australian law — not to GI
  • National funds are held and spent under Australian policies only
  • Website, logo, and public statements must not imply GI ownership or control
  • Constitutional changes require Australian AGM approval — not GI approval

14.2 Shared heritage, separate organisations

Both names reflect Kalenjin diaspora community work. Members may know people in both networks. Gotabgaa International remains a separate US entity. Any cooperation (e.g. attending a cultural event as guests) is optional and must be agreed by the National Board case by case — never assumed.

14.3 External representation

The National President (or board-appointed delegate) represents Gotabgaa Australia only in media, government, and partner meetings. State REPs represent their chapters at national council. No officer is automatically a representative of Gotabgaa International.

  • Chapter Council quorum: majority of recognised chapters represented
  • Constitutional changes: supermajority (e.g. 66%) at AGM with 30-day notice
  • Financial thresholds for benevolence: defined in policy, not ad hoc

16. Dispute Resolution & Conduct

  1. Stage 1: Direct discussion at state level (Chair + parties)
  2. Stage 2: State REP escalates to National Vice President / Coordinator
  3. Stage 3: National Board hearing; Oversight Council advisory input
  4. Stage 4: External mediation or legal advice if required under Australian law

All officers sign a code of conduct: respect, confidentiality for welfare cases, no political campaigning under Gotabgaa name, financial honesty, cultural sensitivity.

18. Australian Legal & Regulatory Context

This section is guidance for leadership planning — not legal advice. Consult an Australian solicitor and accountant before incorporation, fundraising, or registering as a charity.

18.1 Common structural options

OptionDescriptionTypical use for Gotabgaa Australia
Unincorporated federation National body coordinates; states remain separate entities Fast start while states already exist; lower national setup cost
Incorporated association (state) Registered under state law (e.g. NSW, QLD Associations Incorporation Act) Each state chapter or one “lead” state entity as national vehicle
Company limited by guarantee ACNC-registered charity possible; directors, constitution National body with formal governance and public donations
Auspicing Operate programs under an established charity’s umbrella Short-term grants or events before full incorporation

18.2 Regulatory considerations

  • ACNC — if seeking tax-deductible donations, charity registration and ongoing reporting apply
  • Fundraising laws — vary by state; national campaigns may need multi-state compliance
  • Privacy Act — membership databases and welfare cases require data protection policies
  • Working with children — youth programs may require background checks per state law
  • Insurance — public liability for events; directors’ insurance if incorporated

18.3 Recommended governance documents

  1. National constitution (objects, membership, elections, finance, dissolution)
  2. National–state chapter MOU (see Section 24)
  3. Benevolence fund policy and disbursement rules
  4. Code of conduct and conflict-of-interest register
  5. Financial management policy (two signatories, expense limits, audit cycle)
  6. Privacy and welfare confidentiality policy

24. Sample National–State Chapter MOU Outline

Short memorandum between Gotabgaa Australia (national) and [State] Chapter:

  1. Purpose: mutual recognition and cooperation within Gotabgaa Australia
  2. Recognition: state chapter acknowledged as an official chapter of Gotabgaa Australia in [State]
  3. Autonomy: state retains local constitution, elections, local accounts subject to reporting
  4. Branding: use of national logo and tagline per brand guidelines
  5. Reporting: quarterly report to National Coordinator; annual summary to AGM
  6. Finance: state funds remain state-owned; national pooling only by documented transfer
  7. Programs: participation in national programs voluntary unless AGM/council mandates
  8. Representation: Chair and REP attend Chapter Council; REP is voting member where applicable
  9. Disputes: escalation per Section 16 of this framework
  10. Term: MOU renewable annually; termination with 90-day notice and member communication
  11. Signatures: National President, National Secretary General, State Chair, State REP

26. Appendix: Templates & Checklists

26.1 Quarterly state report (to National Coordinator)

  • Chapter name and reporting period
  • Chair and REP names
  • Membership count (full / associate / youth)
  • Events held (date, title, attendance estimate)
  • Welfare cases supported (summary only — no private details)
  • Financial summary: income, expenditure, balance
  • Challenges and support needed from national
  • Proposed events for next quarter

26.2 New chapter recognition checklist

  • ☐ Minimum membership threshold met
  • ☐ Interim Chair and REP named
  • ☐ State constitution or adoption of national template
  • ☐ Bank account or agreed financial arrangement
  • ☐ National Board vote recorded
  • ☐ Listed on website leadership page
  • ☐ Independent status stated on website and materials

26.3 National AGM agenda (template)

  1. Welcome and acknowledgment of country
  2. Minutes of previous AGM
  3. President’s report
  4. Treasurer’s report and budget approval
  5. Coordinator’s program report
  6. State chapter highlights (each REP, 5 minutes)
  7. Elections for vacant board positions
  8. Constitutional amendments (if any)
  9. General business and close
