Governance Structure

Proposed model for national organisation · June 2026

Unity • Heritage • Excellence

Independent Australian association

Foundation: Based on the existing Gotabgaa International By-Laws, the classical structure has three main organs: Membership, Board of Directors, and Oversight & Compliance Council. This document proposes a modern governance model for Gotabgaa Australia that separates governance (Board), management (Executive), and oversight (Council) — considered best practice for large community organisations.

1. Three Core Organs

Membership General Assembly (AGM) — supreme authority
Board of Directors National Council — strategy & policy
Oversight & Compliance Council Independent — ethics, elections, disputes

Management (National Executive Committee) implements Board decisions day-to-day. State chapters deliver programs locally under this national framework.

2. General Assembly (AGM)

Supreme decision-making body Meets annually

Composition

  • All registered and financial members

Powers

  • Elect National Executive
  • Elect Oversight & Compliance Council
  • Amend Constitution
  • Approve annual reports and audited accounts
  • Ratify strategic plans and major policies

Role of the Board

  • Strategic leadership and long-term vision for Gotabgaa Australia
  • Policy formulation (welfare, education, finance, communications, events)
  • Oversight of National Executive — reviews performance at least quarterly
  • Approval of annual budgets, audited accounts, and major projects above set thresholds
  • Appointment of ad hoc committees; receives reports from standing committees
  • Ratification of new state chapter recognition (recommendation from Executive)

Board meeting protocol

  • Quarterly scheduled meetings; extraordinary meetings on Chair or 3 directors’ request
  • Quorum: majority of board members (including at least 2 executive officers + 1 state rep)
  • Agenda circulated 7 days in advance; minutes within 14 days after meeting
  • Executive may attend without vote on items where they are subject of performance review

5. Oversight, Ethics & Compliance Council

Independent organ — separate from Executive and Board Strengthens oversight contemplated in Article 6 (By-Laws reference)

Composition (5 members)

  • Chairperson
  • Secretary
  • Three Council Members

Functions

  • Election supervision and certification of results
  • Dispute resolution per published procedure (Section 20)
  • Disciplinary hearings for breaches of code of conduct
  • Governance audits — annual checklist of compliance with constitution
  • Constitutional interpretation when Board and Executive disagree
  • Conflict of interest investigations; may require officer to step aside temporarily

Term & meetings

  • 3-year terms; members elected at AGM; one renewal permitted (recommended)
  • Minimum bi-annual meetings; extraordinary session on written request of 10+ members
  • Annual report to AGM on elections integrity and dispute statistics (confidential details omitted)
Independence rule: Members cannot simultaneously serve on the Oversight Council and:
  • National Executive Committee
  • Board of Directors
  • State Executive Committees

6. Electoral Commission

Temporary independent body Dissolved after elections conclude

Composition

5–7 members appointed by the Oversight & Compliance Council.

Functions

  • Conduct elections for Executive, Oversight Council, and Board positions as required
  • Vet candidates: membership standing, conduct history, conflict declarations
  • Manage voter register; verify financial membership status before ballot
  • Announce results publicly after Oversight Council certification
  • Preserve sealed ballots or digital audit logs for 12 months

Dissolution: Commission dissolves within 30 days after results certified and officers installed.

7. National Advisory Council (Elders & Founders)

Advisory only — no executive powers

Composition

  • Founders
  • Former National Chairpersons
  • Distinguished community leaders

Role

  • Advisory guidance on culture, heritage, and long-term direction
  • Mediation and mentorship
  • No voting power on Executive or Board decisions

8. National & State Association Relationship

Starting point: Kalenjin community associations already operate at state level (events, welfare, sports days, Taunet Nelel gatherings, city networks). Gotabgaa Australia at national level should unify and strengthen those bodies — not replace, absorb, or override them without consent.

8.1 Recommended model: Federated partnership

The recommended relationship is a federation: each existing state association remains a legal and operational entity in its own right, and joins Gotabgaa Australia as a recognised state chapter through a signed Memorandum of Understanding (MOU). National sets country-wide strategy, brand, and programs; states deliver locally with autonomy in day-to-day affairs.

PrincipleWhat it means in practice
AutonomyState associations keep their own executive, AGM, bank account, and local constitution (or adopt national template)
RecognitionNational Board formally recognises each state as the official Gotabgaa Australia chapter for that state/territory
One national brandPublic face: “Gotabgaa Australia — [State] Chapter” under shared logo and tagline
Dual accountabilityState leaders answer to state members locally and to national policy through state rep on Board
No takeoverNational cannot appoint state chairs or seize state funds without MOU breach and Oversight Council process
Voluntary alignmentStates choose to federate; withdrawal possible with notice per MOU (see 8.8)

8.2 Relationship models — comparison

ModelDescriptionProsConsFit for Gotabgaa Australia?
A. Federation (recommended) National + autonomous state chapters linked by MOU Respects existing groups; clear roles; scalable Requires coordination discipline Yes — best fit
B. Umbrella / affiliate States remain independent; national is loose coordinating forum only Minimal friction; easy start Weak national programs; duplicate events; unclear brand Short-term only
C. Merger / absorption States dissolve into one national entity with branches Single legal body; simple finances Resistance from established states; loss of local identity Not recommended unless states vote to merge
D. Service provider National provides IT, insurance, templates; states pay fee Clear commercial-style boundaries Can feel transactional; less community ownership Optional add-on within federation

8.4 What national offers existing state associations

  • Unified identity — one national logo, website, and “Gotabgaa Australia” presence
  • National programs — education, benevolence framework, cultural calendar, youth/women initiatives
  • Voice at scale — representation in national forums, multicultural councils, partnerships
  • Coordination — avoid clashing dates; share best practices across states
  • Templates — constitution, financial policy, welfare forms, AGM agendas
  • Escalation path — national benevolence when cases exceed state capacity
  • Training — onboarding for new state officers; governance workshops

8.5 What state associations offer the national body

  • Legitimacy — national exists because states choose to federate
  • Local delivery — programs and welfare happen in states, not from a remote national office
  • State Board seat — state rep brings local priorities into national decisions
  • Quarterly reporting — membership, events, finances (summary)
  • Implementation — national policies executed by volunteers on the ground
  • Host rotation — states may host national cultural events (e.g. Taunet Nelel)

8.6 Membership relationship (suggested options)

OptionHow it worksBest when
Option 1 — Single national membership (recommended) Members join Gotabgaa Australia; automatically members of their state chapter; one fee split or allocated per policy Clear national strength; simpler welfare rules
Option 2 — Dual registration Separate state fee + optional national fee; national benefits only if national fee paid States want financial independence during transition
Option 3 — State-only with national associate Primary membership with state; national lists “recognised chapter members” without separate fee initially Early federation phase (first 12 months)

AGM voting rights should be defined in the national constitution (typically financial members of Gotabgaa Australia).

8.7 Financial relationship

  • Separate accounts — state money stays in state accounts; national money in national accounts
  • No automatic pooling — transfers only with written approval (dual signatories + Board if above threshold)
  • Optional national levy — e.g. small % of state fundraising or fixed annual chapter contribution (AGM-approved)
  • National grants to states — for agreed programs (e.g. youth camp); milestone-based disbursement
  • Shared events — revenue split agreed before event (host state vs national program fund)

8.8 MOU essentials (national ↔ each state)

Each recognised state chapter signs an MOU with Gotabgaa Australia covering:

  1. Recognition as official chapter for [State/Territory]
  2. Autonomy of state executive and state AGM
  3. Adoption of national brand guidelines and welfare policy minimums
  4. Quarterly reporting to National Executive
  5. State representative attendance at National Board / Chapter Council
  6. Financial separation and any levy or grant terms
  7. Dispute escalation (state → national → Oversight Council)
  8. Term: renewable annually; withdrawal with 90-day notice and member communication
  9. Signatures: National President, Secretary General, State Chair, State Secretary

13. Detailed Position Descriptions

Key duties for national roles. Final wording should appear in constitution and job descriptions.

12.1 National Chairperson

  • Chief spokesperson; chairs Board and Executive meetings
  • Sets meeting agendas with Secretary General; represents organisation at major events
  • Signs official documents with Secretary General or Treasurer as required
  • Leads strategic planning; ensures non-partisan community focus

12.2 Deputy Chairperson

  • Acts for Chairperson when absent; leads assigned portfolios
  • Supports succession planning and deputy training across chapters
  • Mediates inter-chapter coordination issues before escalation

12.3 Secretary General

  • Maintains constitution, policies, minutes, and official correspondence
  • Manages membership register (or oversees Membership Director)
  • Issues AGM notices; coordinates with Electoral Commission during elections
  • Safe storage of records (digital archive with backup)

12.4 Treasurer

  • Day-to-day banking; payment authorisation per financial policy
  • Monthly reconciliations; quarterly reports to Board
  • Annual budget preparation with Executive; supports audit process
  • Two-signatory rule for payments above threshold (e.g. $5,000 — set in policy)

12.5 Communications Director

  • Website, email, social media aligned with national brand guidelines
  • Press enquiries routed through Chairperson; no unauthorised statements
  • National newsletter minimum quarterly

12.6 Welfare Director

  • Oversees benevolence policy; confidential case handling
  • Coordinates with state welfare coordinators; escalates per policy thresholds
  • Never discusses individual cases in open meetings

12.7 Programs & Events Director

  • Maintains national events calendar; avoids date clashes across states
  • Standards for Taunet Nelel, sports days, AGMs, cultural programs
  • Post-event reports: attendance, finances, lessons learned

12.8 Membership Director

  • Recruitment campaigns; onboarding kit for new members
  • Tracks financial vs non-financial members; renewal reminders
  • Reports membership growth quarterly to Executive and Board

12.9 Portfolio directors (Education, Business, Youth, Women on Board)

Lead respective standing committees; present annual portfolio plans to Board; coordinate state chapter volunteers in their area.

16. Elections Process

  1. Timeline: Oversight Council announces election date at least 60 days before AGM
  2. Nominations: Open period 30 days; candidates must be financial members 12+ months (configurable)
  3. Vetting: Electoral Commission checks eligibility, conflicts, code of conduct acceptance
  4. Campaign: Fair access; no misuse of organisation resources for one candidate
  5. Voting: Secret ballot at AGM or approved electronic vote with audit trail
  6. Results: Electoral Commission certifies; Oversight Council publishes within 48 hours
  7. Handover: Outgoing officers transfer records within 14 days
  8. Terms: 2–3 years staggered; maximum two consecutive terms (recommended)

19. State Chapter Standards

Operational detail for recognised chapters under the federation model (Section 8).

18.1 Recognition criteria

  • Events held and attendance estimates
  • Financial summary (income, expenditure, balance)
  • Welfare cases supported (summary only — no private details)

22. Code of Conduct & Conflict of Interest

21.1 All officers and volunteers must:

  • Treat members with respect; zero tolerance for harassment or discrimination
  • Keep welfare and personal data confidential
  • Not use Gotabgaa name for personal political campaigning
  • Declare conflicts of interest before relevant votes (written register updated annually)
  • Separate personal business from organisation transactions

21.2 Conflict examples requiring declaration

  • Family member applying for benevolence or scholarship
  • Vendor owned by an officer receiving contract
  • Dual role in another organisation with competing interests
